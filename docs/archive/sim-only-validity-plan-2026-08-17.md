# Initial Sim-Only Validity Plan

> **Document status:** Historical planning snapshot. The production 10 mm SCM
> A0 and CPIC Genesis bridge have since completed; use the companion
> `tera_splat/docs/current-state.md` for the current evidence and readiness
> gate.

Snapshot date: 2026-08-17

## 1. Purpose

This plan defines the smallest simulator-only experiment needed to determine
whether the proposed Chrono to Genesis MPM to Gaussian terrain pipeline is
technically valid before performing hardware experiments.

The experiment is intentionally narrower than the complete project. It tests
three linked questions:

1. Can Genesis MPM reproduce the observable terrain deformation generated by a
   controlled Chrono SCM episode?
2. Does the fitted Genesis model generalize to at least one unseen simulated
   load or contact location?
3. Can the Genesis displacement and deformation-gradient fields update one
   initial Gaussian scene into a geometrically consistent deformed scene
   without retraining?

The experiment does not attempt to validate real sand, dynamic quadruped
walking, ROS 2 integration, or a complete terrain-aware planner.

## 2. Validity claim being tested

The strongest claim supported by a successful experiment is:

> A Genesis MPM surrogate can be fitted to reproduce selected observable
> surface responses from a controlled Chrono SCM oracle, generalize to held-out
> synthetic actions, and drive a persistent Gaussian terrain representation
> into a consistent counterfactual deformed state without retraining.

This remains a synthetic cross-model result. Chrono SCM is not physical ground
truth, and success does not establish that the fitted Genesis parameters
describe real sand.

## 3. Current implemented starting point

The current Chrono repository already provides:

- PyChrono 8 with corrected SCM friction-angle handling;
- explicit smoke and full-resolution terrain modes;
- stationary initial, loaded, and residual height-map output;
- open-loop Go1 traversal with independent foot contacts;
- PyVista/VTK rendering and signed DEM visualization;
- calibrated multi-ring RGB-D orbit capture;
- direct export of known RGB-D poses and geometry to COLMAP;
- a one-iteration Frankenstein Gaussian training smoke test;
- 29 passing unit tests.

The current repository does not yet provide:

- a canonical Chrono episode exporter for Genesis;
- a Genesis MPM version of the same terrain and action;
- material-parameter fitting across the two simulators;
- an MPM-particle-to-Gaussian deformation adapter;
- rendered validation of the deformed Gaussian scene.

## 4. Scope freeze

### 4.1 Included

- flat, bounded terrain bed;
- one gravity-loaded rigid object;
- independent reset for every action;
- initial, loaded, and residual surface states;
- full-resolution Chrono reference data;
- Genesis parameter fitting;
- at least one held-out simulated action;
- initial Gaussian reconstruction from calibrated synthetic RGB-D;
- Gaussian mean and covariance updates from Genesis;
- geometry and rendered-depth evaluation.

### 4.2 Excluded

- articulated Go1 dynamics;
- balance or locomotion control;
- rolling-terrain traversal as the first fitting episode;
- reduced-order hazard and fall behavior;
- ROS 2;
- costmap integration;
- sequential Chrono-to-Genesis hidden-state transfer;
- individual-grain rendering;
- appearance synthesis for newly exposed sand;
- hardware data collection;
- claims about calibrated real material properties.

The existing rolling-terrain Go1 maneuver may be used as a qualitative final
demonstration only after the controlled stationary experiment passes.

## 5. Minimal experimental object and action

Use a single rigid cylinder for the first cross-simulator comparison. It is
easier to reproduce in Chrono and Genesis than a four-foot robot proxy and has
a direct later hardware equivalent.

Initial cylinder specification:

| Property | Value |
|---|---:|
| Diameter | `0.14605 m` / `5.75 in` |
| Height | `0.0508 m` / `2.0 in` |
| Nominal mass | `1.5 kg` |
| Initial XY | center of the bed |
| Loading mode | released under gravity |
| Removal | remove after the loaded state is recorded |

The action must not prescribe penetration depth. The cylinder starts from a
known clearance, is released under gravity, settles, is measured, and is then
removed for residual-state measurement.

If the cylinder has not yet been implemented in the current Chrono repository,
add it as a separate stationary action. Do not repurpose the traversal gait or
four-foot contact implementation for this test.

## 6. Episode set

Use a small deterministic episode set:

| Episode | Action | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `A0_repeat` | nominal mass, center | Chrono determinism check |
| `A0_cal` | nominal mass, center | Genesis parameter fitting |
| `A1_mass` | different mass, center | Held-out load test |
| `A2_offset` | nominal mass, fixed XY offset | Held-out location test |

Recommended simulation-only choices are:

- nominal mass: `1.5 kg`;
- alternate mass: either `0.75 kg` or `3.0 kg`;
- offset: `0.15 m` along one horizontal axis while remaining away from the SCM
  boundary artifact.

The final hardware protocol may replace the alternate mass with the second
available physical weight. The episode schema must therefore store mass and
pose explicitly rather than relying on fixed episode names.

Only `A0_cal` may be used to fit Genesis in the initial validity test.
`A1_mass` and `A2_offset` must remain frozen until final evaluation.

## 7. Observable state definition

The prediction interface is:

\[
(S_0,A) \longrightarrow (\hat S_L,\hat S_R),
\]

where:

- \(S_0\) is the initial metric surface;
- \(A\) is the rigid-object geometry, mass, pose, gravity, and timing;
- \(S_L\) is the loaded surface;
- \(S_R\) is the residual surface after removal.

Use only quantities that can later be observed or reproduced in hardware:

- metric surface elevation;
- rigid-object geometry;
- mass;
- contact position and orientation;
- loaded and removal timing;
- object sinkage and pose;
- rendered RGB and depth.

Chrono internal SCM state may be saved for debugging, but it must not be a
required input to Genesis or to the eventual hardware pipeline.

## 8. Canonical episode output

Each Chrono episode should produce:

```text
episode_<id>/
|-- manifest.yaml
|-- action.json
|-- initial_heightmap_m.npy
|-- loaded_heightmap_m.npy
|-- residual_heightmap_m.npy
|-- valid_heightmap_mask.npy
|-- object_pose.csv
|-- metrics.json
`-- rgbd/
    |-- initial/
    |-- loaded/
    `-- residual/
```

Required metadata:

- world and `bed` frame definitions;
- array axis order and origin;
- grid spacing and bounds;
- time step and sample times;
- cylinder dimensions, mass, inertia, and initial pose;
- gravity;
- complete SCM parameters;
- smoke or full-resolution status;
- boundary mask definition;
- software versions and code revision.

The RGB-D directories are optional for initial cross-simulator fitting but are
required for the Gaussian stage.

## 9. Phase A: Chrono reference episodes

### 9.1 Configuration

Use the configured `10 mm` SCM grid, not the `35-40 mm` smoke grid, for all
reported validity results. Smoke mode may be used only for plumbing tests.

Start with:

- the current corrected friction-angle handling;
- bulldozing disabled;
- flat initial terrain;
- the cylinder action defined in Section 5;
- a fresh Chrono world for every episode.

Bulldozing introduces an additional behavior and should be tested only after
the no-bulldozing comparison is understood.

### 9.2 Recorded states

For every action:

1. Build a fresh Chrono system and terrain.
2. Sample \(H_0^C\).
3. release the cylinder under gravity.
4. Wait for the existing settled-state condition.
5. Record the loaded cylinder pose and \(H_L^C\).
6. Remove the cylinder.
7. Advance the configured residual-settling interval.
8. Record \(H_R^C\).
9. Save all arrays and metadata.

### 9.3 Chrono validity checks

- all height maps have the expected full-resolution shape;
- all valid values are finite;
- the boundary ring is excluded consistently;
- repeated `A0` runs agree within numerical tolerance;
- increased mass does not produce less maximum sinkage under the same setup;
- loaded deformation is spatially centered under the known contact;
- residual deformation does not exceed loaded deformation without an explained
  uplift or bulldozing mechanism.

Exit condition: the Chrono reference episodes are deterministic and physically
ordered enough to serve as synthetic targets.

## 10. Phase B: Genesis reproduction

### 10.1 Initial bed

Construct a volumetric Genesis bed from \(H_0^C\):

\[
z_{\mathrm{base}} \le z \le H_0^C(x,y).
\]

Requirements:

- use the same horizontal bounds and metric frame as Chrono;
- fill the material volume below the surface;
- use a fixed or constrained base;
- use equivalent lateral containment;
- settle the bed under gravity before loading;
- save the settled initial particle state;
- reset to that state for every parameter evaluation.

Do not use only surface particles or visible Gaussians as the MPM material.

### 10.1.1 Settling-stage contract

The settling stage is a separate state-preparation experiment, not a material
fit.  Its only output eligible for Phase C is an **accepted prepared-bed
artifact** containing the particle surface, ground-plane metadata, and the
complete MPM state:

```text
pos, vel, C, F, Jp, active
```

For the initial 40 mm smoke bridge, the acceptance gates are frozen as:

```text
all-bed particle-speed p99 <= 0.0005 m/s for a continuous 0.02 s
RMSE(H0^M, H0^C) <= 5 mm on the common valid mask
max_abs(H0^M - H0^C) <= 10 mm on the common valid mask
```

The artifact manifest must record: Chrono episode path and hash, heightmap
frame/mask, particle spacing and bed depth, ground and lateral constraints,
Genesis/material version, `dt`, gravity, geostatic initialization method and
all parameters, settling duration and termination reason, and the two surface
errors.  A failed artifact is retained as evidence but is ineligible as a
Phase-C initial condition.

Particle spacing and MPM particle volume are a coupled physical setting.  For
a regular lattice, record both the lattice spacing and `particle_size`, and
verify that the implied particle volume/mass is consistent with the filled-bed
volume.  Do not use a sparse lattice with a much smaller particle volume: it
can make a visually present bed too light to support the rigid object.  Record
whether CPIC rigid--MPM coupling is enabled; it is likewise frozen with the
prepared state and replayed for every candidate.

Every material candidate must rebuild the same scene and restore this complete
state before the cylinder is placed.  The following are frozen for one
BayesOpt campaign and are **not** optimization dimensions: the prepared-bed
identifier, particle/grid resolution, `dt`, density, Poisson ratio, gravity,
cylinder geometry/mass/clearance, containment, contact settings, surface
projection, valid mask, loaded/post-settle rules, and removal protocol.
Changing any of them starts a named new settling/configuration campaign; it
does not extend the existing optimization history.  Never vertically shift
particles, add numerical damping, or loosen the settling gate to create a
candidate state.

### 10.2 Matching the action

Genesis must use the same:

- cylinder dimensions;
- cylinder mass and inertia;
- initial pose and clearance;
- gravity;
- release condition;
- loaded-state criterion or sample time;
- removal time;
- residual settling duration.

Record any difference that cannot be matched exactly between the simulator
APIs.

### 10.3 Surface extraction

Extract a Genesis surface \(H^M\) on the Chrono grid. For the initial test:

1. bin particles in XY;
2. select the highest occupied particle in each bin;
3. fill only small interior holes;
4. resample onto the Chrono XY coordinates;
5. apply the same valid mask used for Chrono metrics.

Keep the raw particle state so the surface-extraction method can be changed
without rerunning every simulation.

## 11. Phase C: effective parameter fitting

### 11.1 Initial parameter set

Fit only a small effective parameter set:

\[
\theta = \{\log E,\phi\},
\]

where \(E\) is an effective stiffness parameter and \(\phi\) is the Genesis
friction angle. Hold density, Poisson ratio, bed depth, grid resolution, and
contact settings fixed during the initial test.

The installed Genesis material API must be checked before implementation. Do
not assume that similarly named Chrono and Genesis parameters have identical
physical meanings.

### 11.2 Calibration loss

Compare deformation relative to the initial surface:

\[
D_L^C = H_L^C-H_0^C,
\qquad
D_L^M = H_L^M-H_0^M.
\]

Use:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cal}}(\theta)
=
w_h\operatorname{RMSE}(D_L^M,D_L^C)
+w_s\left|s^M-s^C\right|
+w_v\left|V^M-V^C\right|
+w_d\left|d_{\max}^M-d_{\max}^C\right|.
\]

Here:

- \(s\) is cylinder sinkage;
- \(V\) is signed displaced volume over the valid region;
- \(d_{\max}\) is maximum surface depression.

Do not include held-out episodes in this loss. Report residual-state agreement
separately first; add it to the fitting loss only in a documented second
experiment.

### 11.3 Search procedure

For initial validity:

1. define physically stable bounds for \(\log E\) and \(\phi\);
2. run a coarse grid or low-discrepancy search;
3. discard unstable or invalid simulations with an explicit penalty;
4. refine the best region using Bayesian optimization;
5. retain the complete optimization history;
6. freeze the best parameter pair before opening held-out results.

Approximately 25-40 valid Genesis evaluations are sufficient for the initial
two-parameter test. This is an engineering fit, not a uniqueness or material-
identification proof.

### 11.4 BayesOpt campaign contract

Use BayesOpt only after an accepted prepared-bed artifact is named in the
campaign manifest.  The initial campaign has exactly two continuous dimensions:

```text
log10_E in [4.0, 6.0]
phi_deg in [15.0, 45.0]
```

Run a deterministic, seeded low-discrepancy warm start of 10 candidates,
followed by expected-improvement proposals until 30 valid candidates have
completed (or the fixed compute budget is exhausted).  A Gaussian-process
surrogate should use normalized coordinates and a recorded acquisition seed.
The optimizer may propose a point only once; duplicate rounded candidates are
resampled before execution.

Each evaluation must run only `A0_cal`, restore the campaign's accepted MPM
state, and write its own immutable directory.  The objective is the frozen
loaded-state loss in Section 11.2.  A simulation is **invalid**, rather than a
high-loss physical observation, if it fails the prepared-state identity check,
loses valid-mask coverage, has non-finite maps, violates the loaded/post-settle
criteria, exceeds the containment/domain checks, or fails to complete the
required removal protocol.  Record its status and diagnostic reason, exclude
it from the GP fit, and use a fixed explicit acquisition penalty greater than
every feasible loss.  Do not silently substitute a partial/capped smoke run
for a valid evaluation.

The campaign index must contain, per proposal:

```text
proposal index and seed
log10_E, E, phi_deg
prepared-bed manifest digest and frozen-config digest
run path, exit/status, wall time, GPU/device
all settling/containment diagnostics
valid-mask count and loaded/residual metrics
loss components and total loss (or invalid reason/penalty)
surrogate/acquisition values and parent history revision
```

Freeze the selected pair and its exact prepared-bed/config digests before
opening `A1_mass` or `A2_offset`.  A BayesOpt campaign that repeatedly selects
a bound is a diagnostic failure requiring inspection of the action, contact,
and loss conventions—not an invitation to add more material parameters.

## 12. Phase D: held-out synthetic evaluation

Run the frozen Genesis parameters on:

- `A1_mass`: unseen mass at the center;
- `A2_offset`: nominal mass at an unseen location.

Report:

- loaded height-map RMSE;
- residual height-map RMSE;
- maximum sinkage error;
- deformation-radius error;
- displaced-volume error;
- radial surface-profile error;
- runtime and failure rate.

Required baselines:

1. no-deformation prediction \(\hat H_L=H_0\);
2. Genesis with its initial untuned parameters;
3. Genesis with the calibrated parameters.

The calibrated model must be evaluated with no changes to its parameters,
surface extraction, masks, or loss definition.

## 13. Phase E: initial Gaussian scene

### 13.1 Required states

Capture calibrated RGB-D orbits for:

- \(S_0\): initial undeformed terrain;
- \(S_L^C\): Chrono loaded target;
- optionally \(S_R^C\): Chrono residual target.

The current capture path records one final state. Add either:

- an explicit `initial`, `loaded`, or `residual` capture selector; or
- a deterministic saved-state renderer that renders the stored height maps and
  cylinder pose using the same camera definitions.

Use identical cameras for all compared states. Hide the robot or cylinder for
terrain-only geometry evaluation, and keep an additional visible-object render
only for presentation.

### 13.2 Gaussian training

1. Export the initial RGB-D dataset directly to COLMAP using the known poses.
2. Train one Gaussian scene \(G_0\) from \(S_0\).
3. Confirm metric alignment with the `bed` frame.
4. Select terrain Gaussians using the known terrain bounds and a surface-height
   band.
5. Keep rigid floor, tray, background, and object Gaussians outside the
   deformable set.

The loaded and residual datasets are evaluation targets. They must not be used
to retrain the predicted Gaussian state.

An independently trained loaded-state Gaussian may be produced as a rendering
upper bound, but it must be labeled as an oracle reconstruction rather than a
prediction.

## 14. Phase F: Genesis to Gaussian transfer

Following the kinematic update used in PhysGaussian, interpolate the MPM state
onto the visible terrain Gaussians rather than treating the reconstructed
surface alone as the simulated volume.

For each selected terrain Gaussian \(g\), interpolate displacement and
deformation gradient from neighboring MPM particles:

\[
\boldsymbol{u}_g
=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal{N}(g)}
w_{gp}\left(\boldsymbol{x}'_p-\boldsymbol{x}_p\right),
\]

\[
\boldsymbol{F}_g
=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal{N}(g)}w_{gp}\boldsymbol{F}_p.
\]

Update the Gaussian mean and covariance:

\[
\boldsymbol{\mu}'_g
=
\boldsymbol{\mu}_g+\boldsymbol{u}_g,
\]

\[
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}'_g
=
\boldsymbol{F}_g
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g
\boldsymbol{F}_g^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Use the rotational component of \(\boldsymbol{F}_g\) to update the local
orientation used by the spherical-harmonic appearance representation.

### 14.1 Gaussian ablations

Evaluate:

1. undeformed \(G_0\);
2. mean displacement only;
3. mean plus rigid covariance rotation;
4. mean plus full covariance deformation.

Keep opacity and appearance coefficients fixed for the first experiment. This
isolates geometric validity from appearance synthesis.

## 15. Evaluation metrics

### 15.1 Chrono to Genesis

- height-change RMSE and MAE;
- peak sinkage error;
- deformation radius at fixed depth thresholds;
- signed negative and positive volume;
- radial-profile RMSE;
- residual-to-loaded deformation ratio;
- runtime per episode.

### 15.2 Genesis to Gaussian

- rendered depth RMSE against the Genesis surface;
- rendered depth RMSE against the Chrono target;
- point-to-surface or Chamfer distance in the deforming region;
- deformation-boundary discontinuity;
- invalid covariance count;
- rendered PSNR and SSIM as secondary appearance metrics;
- Gaussian update and rendering runtime.

Depth and geometric metrics are primary. Image quality alone cannot establish
correct terrain deformation.

## 16. Provisional pass criteria

The initial test passes when all of the following hold:

1. Repeated Chrono `A0` episodes agree within the defined numerical tolerance.
2. The calibrated Genesis model reduces loaded deformation RMSE on `A0_cal` by
   at least 50% relative to the untuned Genesis model.
3. On both held-out actions, the calibrated Genesis prediction improves over
   the no-deformation baseline by at least 20% in deformation RMSE.
4. Held-out maximum-sinkage error is no greater than 30% of the Chrono target
   sinkage.
5. The deformed Gaussian depth prediction improves over undeformed \(G_0\) by
   at least 30% in the affected region.
6. The Gaussian update produces no non-finite means or covariances and no
   visually explosive Gaussian scales.
7. The full-covariance update is at least as geometrically accurate as the
   mean-only update, or any degradation is isolated and explained.

These are provisional engineering gates, not publication-level universal
thresholds. Record the grid spacing and target deformation magnitude beside
every reported error.

## 17. Failure interpretation

| Failure | Likely interpretation | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Chrono repeats disagree | Source episode is not trustworthy | Fix reset, settling, or sampling before Genesis work |
| Genesis cannot fit `A0_cal` | Frame/action mismatch, surface-extraction error, or constitutive mismatch | Diagnose interfaces before adding parameters |
| Genesis fits calibration but fails both holdouts | Effective parameters are overfit or non-identifiable | Add one calibration action or stronger parameter constraints |
| Mass holdout passes but offset fails | Boundary or coordinate dependence | Inspect containment and valid mask |
| MPM surface matches but Gaussian depth fails | Particle-to-Gaussian mapping is incorrect | Test mapping on an analytic deformation field |
| Mean update works but covariance fails | Deformation-gradient convention or covariance update is incorrect | Validate \(F\) with rigid and uniform-scale test cases |
| Geometry matches but RGB quality degrades | Fixed appearance is insufficient | Separate appearance modeling from mechanical validity |
| All simulation stages pass | Sim-only concept is valid | Freeze the pipeline and begin hardware protocol |

Do not respond to a failed two-parameter fit by immediately exposing many more
material parameters. First verify units, frames, contact geometry, action
timing, surface extraction, and loss normalization.

## 19. Implementation order

1. Add the stationary cylinder action to Chrono.
2. Add renderer-independent canonical episode export.
3. Generate and verify `A0_repeat`, `A0_cal`, `A1_mass`, and `A2_offset`.
4. Build and settle the matching Genesis bed.
5. Match the cylinder action and surface extraction.
6. Run the two-parameter calibration.
7. Freeze parameters and evaluate held-out actions.
8. Add initial/loaded/residual RGB-D capture using identical cameras.
9. Train the initial Gaussian scene.
10. Implement mean-only MPM-to-Gaussian transfer.
11. Add covariance and orientation transfer.
12. Run ablations and apply the pass criteria.
13. Write a short validity report before expanding to traversal or hardware.

## 20. Stop conditions

Stop and diagnose before continuing when:

- Chrono results are not repeatable;
- a simulator uses a different action or coordinate frame;
- Genesis particles escape the intended bed bounds;
- surface extraction changes the measured result substantially;
- optimization selects a parameter bound repeatedly;
- held-out data is accidentally used during fitting;
- the Gaussian map is trained on the target loaded state;
- geometry evaluation is replaced with only RGB image metrics.

## 21. Hardware transition after validity

After the sim-only pipeline passes, retain the same interface and replace the
Chrono target with D455 observations:

\[
S_0^R,A
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{real\ experiment}}
S_L^R,S_R^R.
\]

The first real test should report both:

1. frozen zero-shot evaluation using the simulator-developed pipeline; and
2. limited real calibration using one episode, followed by evaluation on the
   remaining mass or location without retuning.

Real captures must use the fixed AprilTag `bed` frame, `tag36h11` IDs 30-33,
the known `0.1728 m` tag width, and before/loaded/post-removal D455 states.

Hardware validation is a later evidence stage. No part of the initial sim-only
validity experiment should be described as real-sand validation.

## 22. Agent completion checklist

Before declaring the initial validity test complete, verify:

- full-resolution Chrono data was used for reported results;
- every action started from a fresh or verified reset state;
- Chrono and Genesis used the same cylinder geometry and action;
- height maps share a documented metric frame and valid mask;
- Genesis used volumetric particles beneath the surface;
- only `A0_cal` influenced fitted parameters;
- held-out mass and offset results were generated after parameter freezing;
- the initial Gaussian was trained only from \(S_0\);
- loaded and residual Gaussian states were produced without retraining;
- mean-only and covariance-update ablations were reported;
- geometry metrics were reported in addition to image metrics;
- output claims identify Chrono as the synthetic oracle;
- limitations and failed cases are included in `report.md`.

## 23. Reference

The Gaussian deformation equations are based on the representation update in:

> Xie et al., "PhysGaussian: Physics-Integrated 3D Gaussians for Generative
> Dynamics," arXiv:2311.12198v3, 2024.

The project reuses the Gaussian displacement, covariance, and orientation
ideas. It does not assume that the paper's custom MPM implementation or
manually selected material settings are equivalent to Genesis MPM.
